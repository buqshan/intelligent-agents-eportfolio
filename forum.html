Section 03

Forum Contributions and Analysis

My initial post used telecom incident management as its case. An agent takes the customer's report, logs the ticket, commits to the SLA, diagnoses the root cause and remediates by patching, rebooting or re-addressing. I mapped it to PEAS (Russell and Norvig, 2021): SLA adherence as the performance measure, the service platform and network as the environment, ticketing and configuration APIs as actuators, and alarms and customer messages as sensors.

My four peer reviews urged colleagues to explain agent properties rather than list them, name BDI explicitly (Rao and Georgeff, 1995) and pair limitations with mitigations such as contract nets (Sycara, 1998).

The feedback I received was more valuable. Yasmin warned that an agent rebooting infrastructure on a wrong diagnosis could worsen an incident and called for human-in-the-loop control (Amershi et al., 2019; NIST, 2023). Luis added goal misalignment and miscoordination. Fedi reframed coordination as resilience and asked for before-and-after resolution figures.

Analysis. The peers converged on the weakest claim in my post: that the whole lifecycle could run "often without human involvement". My summary post moved to autonomy bounded by goal specification and oversight. Two gaps remain. I never supplied the quantitative evidence Fedi requested, and my initial post cited Wooldridge (2018) and Russell and Norvig (2022) although the editions I used were 2009 and 2021. I corrected the dates in the summary post, but the slip shows I had trusted generated citations without checking them.
